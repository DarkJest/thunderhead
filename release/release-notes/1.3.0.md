# Thunderhead 1.3.0 — "The Sky Is Alive"

A storm used to be rain, a wait, a bolt, a bang, repeat. Now it is a system: it flickers inside its
own clouds, sends discharges travelling sideways through them, and only sometimes puts one on the
ground.

### Lightning is no longer one algorithm with randomised parameters

Every flash is now one of five things, and which one it is decides its geometry, its timeline, its
colour, how much it lights the cloud around it and how hard it thunders.

- **Positive superbolts.** Rare, and immediately a different event rather than a brighter one: a
  channel half again as wide, roughly a fifth of the branching, a violet halo instead of a cyan one,
  and a single dominant stroke that holds for the better part of a second instead of stuttering three
  times. About one strike in twenty-five, and adjustable.
- The archetype is chosen from the strike's own seed, so every player in a session sees the same one
  without a single extra packet.

### The storm itself is electrically alive

- **Cloud-to-cloud lightning.** Channels that travel horizontally through the storm, advancing in
  legs that change heading and altitude, branching flat instead of hanging downward, and never
  touching the ground. Thunder is scheduled from several points along the channel, so a long one
  arrives as a roll rather than a clap.
- **Intracloud lightning.** Discharges buried inside a cloud: barely any visible channel, and the
  cloud pulsing several times from within instead. A storm on the horizon is now busy without
  producing a single ground strike.
- **Megaflashes.** Kilometre-scale horizontal events that are visibly seen to propagate for over a
  second across most of the visible storm. Roughly one per several hundred ambient discharges.
- **Cloud illumination.** Clouds light up from the inside around a discharge, as irregular pulsing
  regions rather than a global sky flash.

### Above the storm

Two things that are not lightning at all, and are not drawn as it.

- **Red sprites.** The enormous red structures that appear far above a thunderstorm moments after a
  powerful positive discharge, and are gone in a fraction of a second. Built from the real
  morphology — a cluster of elements, each a diffuse head over a short bright body with tendrils
  combed downward and outward, and the faint cool fringe reaching up above them. Raised only over a
  discharge far enough away to be looked at rather than stood under.
- **Blue jets.** Cones of violet-blue light climbing out of a cloud top and splitting near the end of
  the climb. Slow enough that the rise itself is the effect, where a sprite is a flash.

Both are rare on purpose. Seeing one should be the reason you start recording, not something the sky
does every minute.

### Settings

A new **sky** group in the settings screen: activity master switch and pace, cloud-to-cloud,
intracloud, cloud illumination and its strength, superbolt rate, red sprites and blue jets.
`config/tempestfx.json` additionally exposes megaflash rarity, sprite and jet rates, and hard caps on
ambient channels, lit cloud regions and events above the storm.

**Reduced flashing** switches off intracloud pulse trains, cloud illumination, superbolts, sprites
and jets, and slows ambient activity to a third. Everything new obeys it.

### For other mods

`DischargeType` is public. `LightningEffect.builder().type(...)` asks Thunderhead for a specific
archetype, and leaving it unset lets Thunderhead choose one from the seed as before.
`LightningStrikeFxEvent.dischargeType()` reports what a strike turned out to be. Code written against
1.2 keeps compiling.

### Unchanged, on purpose

The ordinary negative cloud-to-ground flash is exactly the bolt it was. Every new archetype is
calibrated against it rather than replacing it, and the rendering path, the compositor and the shader
independence are untouched — the effect still looks the same with a shader pack as without one.

Nothing new touches the server. Ambient discharges, cloud illumination, sprites and jets are all
client-side ambience: no damage, no block changes, no packets, and nothing to install on a server.

### Trying the new events on purpose

They are rare by design, so there are commands for looking at one deliberately:

```
/tempestfx sky cloud_to_cloud|intracloud|megaflash|positive_cloud_to_ground [distance]
/tempestfx aloft red_sprite|blue_jet [distance]
```

`general.debug` adds storm charge, live aerial channels, lit cloud regions and sprites to the
overlay.

### Tested

Minecraft 1.21.1, Fabric and NeoForge, vanilla rendering. 226 automated tests cover the geometry,
the timelines, the storm model, the ambient scheduler, sprite and jet morphology, the public API and
config validation.

Two of those tests exist because of bugs found by playing rather than by reading: a wide channel with
unchanged subdivision draws quads that overlap at sharp angles instead of joining, and a bare bright
trunk shows the seams between them where a branched one hides them. Both are now measured on real
generated geometry, for every archetype, on every build.
